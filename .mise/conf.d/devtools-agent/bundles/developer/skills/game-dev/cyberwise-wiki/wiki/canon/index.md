# Canon

What Cyberpunk 2077 actually establishes, what it deliberately leaves open, and
the craft of writing a character into it.

**Most of this exists because it was got wrong first.** Nearly every article here
records a confabulation alongside its correction - a number assumed to be a
player's invention that turned out to be on screen, a friendship invented and
retracted, a wiki date that resolves to a developer saying there is no date. The
wrong road is kept on purpose: it is the half that stops the next reader walking
it.

Three rules run through the whole area, and they are worth reading before anything
else here:

- **[Trace the footnote before repeating the wiki](/canon/trace-the-footnote-before-repeating-the-wiki)** -
  a wiki renders a documented fact and a fan's subtraction in the same font. Find
  what the claim rests on, and say "derived, not documented" when that is the
  answer.
- **[Where lifepath canon actually lives](/canon/where-lifepath-canon-lives)** -
  four primary sources, in order, and the standing rule that follows from having
  them: do not introduce lore that is not grounded in an in-game source.
- **[Before calling something non-canonical, search the mail and the shards](/canon/search-the-mail-and-the-shards-first)** -
  the negative claim is the risky direction, because it closes a question instead
  of inviting a source. "Not that I can source" is the honest form of it, and the
  text is greppable rather than only readable in game.

## What the game establishes

Read off the screen. These are `stable` except where the line says otherwise.

- [The corpo prologue is an assassination and a firing, and V and Jackie are already friends](/canon/the-corpo-prologue) - the employee ID on the bathroom mirror, Jenkins' failed hit on Abernathy, and Lizzie's Bar as the moment V loses the job rather than the moment they meet
- [The streetkid and nomad prologues give you furniture, not a biography](/canon/streetkid-and-nomad-anchors) - Padre, the car job and Jackie; a family situation that has already collapsed and a solo ride into the city
- [Jackie dies before Johnny exists for V, and the two never overlap](/canon/jackie-dies-before-johnny-exists) - the most common ordering error in any retelling from memory
- [The only firm dates near the heist are April and May 2077](/canon/the-dates-the-game-puts-on-the-board) - three anchors, and everything else around the prologue is arithmetic run backwards from them
- [The endings are named for tarot cards, and the point of no return is in the journal](/canon/the-endings-are-named-for-tarot) - there is no canonical ending, and assuming one overwrites the player's character
- [What you do with Jackie's body gates a later sidejob](/canon/what-happens-to-jackies-remains) - family or nowhere unlocks the ofrenda; the ripperdoc does not, because the corporation takes the body from him. Assistant and player were both wrong, and only the game's own text broke the tie
- [A streetkid-only character reappears in content every lifepath sees](/canon/a-lifepath-character-can-reappear-outside-its-lifepath) - he is in the shared montage by clothing, and later dialogue remembers V beating him up *(draft: one observation, and the visual identification is the shaky part)*

## Setting lore

Reasoned from setting material rather than read off a screen. These are `draft`
and say so.

- [Hong Kong is a city of the digitised dead, not an exclusion zone](/canon/hong-kong-the-ghost-world) - the Ghost World, formed in the remnants of the old NET, and why Arasaka would care - marked as inference
- [Arasaka names hardware after warlords, and its American branch is a tenancy](/canon/arasaka-naming-and-the-american-branch) - two heuristics for judging whether an Arasaka-sounding invention is plausible, and the warning against running either backwards into a claim
- [A weapon can exist only in a shard, and killing its owner drops something else](/canon/a-weapon-can-exist-only-in-a-shard) - lore-only naming is a real category, and searching for the item finds nothing because there is nothing

## The tabletop underneath

The setting predates the video game by decades, and a mod claiming to be
"lore-accurate" is usually claiming to be accurate to the tabletop rather than to
2077. That claim is only checkable against the original rules.

- [Humanity in the tabletop is permanent debt, so a renewable meter is a deliberate deviation](/canon/humanity-in-the-tabletop-and-how-mods-deviate) - Empathy x10, a cost paid at install, therapy as the only refund, cyberpsychosis as partly a poverty disease, and the creator's insistence that it is dissociative rather than "metal makes you crazy"

Established by reading the game, and the place where a fiction meets a build.

## Character craft

- [The diegetic answer to "why does my veteran start at level 1"](/canon/why-a-veteran-starts-at-level-one) - the prologue's weakness is an amputation; name what was taken and the mechanic becomes characterisation
- [Match the document format to the world that produced the character](/canon/match-the-document-to-the-world) - corpos exist as surveillance records, streetkids as self-narrated legend, nomads as witnessed accounts
- [Writing an in-world intelligence document that reads as real](/canon/writing-an-in-world-intelligence-document) - go cold to SUBJECT, grade source reliability, write flaws as exploitation vectors, and never tidy a quoted interview
- [Verify the real-world technical detail the fiction leans on](/canon/verify-the-technical-detail-the-fiction-leans-on) - the true version is usually the better premise, and a reader who knows the field trips hard on a wrong one

## What is deliberately not here

**No player's invented lore, however good it is.** A great deal of material that
reads exactly like canon - internal Arasaka systems, named projects, named
directors, first names attached to characters the game leaves surnamed - was
authored by players, and it is not in these articles even as an example, because
an example in a canon area gets quoted as canon within a year.

Where a canon character's first name was never established in game, the
formulation used here is: **the surname is canon and the first name is not.**

The document *shapes* a backstory can take are one area over, in
[the character-document formats](/formats/character-document-formats). This area
covers what goes in them.
