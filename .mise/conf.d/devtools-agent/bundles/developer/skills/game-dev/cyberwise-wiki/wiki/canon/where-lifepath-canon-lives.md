# Where lifepath canon actually lives, in priority order

Most prologue questions are answerable from the game itself in a couple of
minutes. The reason they get answered from memory instead is that nobody writes
down *where to look*. Here it is.

## The four primary sources

**In this order**, because that is roughly the order of both authority and
convenience:

1. **The intro sequence itself.** What is on screen, spoken, or on a HUD readout.
   This is the top of the stack and it settles arguments -
   [V's corpo employee ID](/canon/the-corpo-prologue) is on the bathroom mirror,
   and no amount of speculation outranks that.
2. **The quest detail-bar text.** The journal's own summary of what is happening.
   Frequently states, in flat prose, the thing everyone is arguing about - and it
   is written by CDPR.
3. **The shards scattered around Arasaka Tower.** The corpo prologue is unusually
   well documented because its setting is an organisation that documents things.
   Most people walk past them.
4. **The messages on V's office computer.** Same category, easily missed, and the
   most personal of the four - it is the only place corpo V exists as somebody
   with correspondence.

## The wiki is not a primary source

It is a useful index *to* these, and a genuinely bad substitute for them. It
carries derived numbers in the same typeface as documented ones - see
[Trace the footnote before repeating the wiki](/canon/trace-the-footnote-before-repeating-the-wiki).

The practical form of this: when a wiki page answers a question, use it to find
out **which in-game source it is citing**, then go and read that. If it cites
nothing, you have learned something important.

## The standing rule

**Do not introduce lore that is not grounded in an in-game source.**

Not as background, not as colour, not as a plausible-sounding aside. This is the
rule that fell out of the corpo-prologue failures, and it is narrow and strict on
purpose:

- **Ideas offered as ideas are fine** - explicitly marked, in a clear register
  change, handed back for the player to take or bin.
- **A statement about the world is not fine** unless you can name the source.
- **"I do not know, and here is where we would find out"** is a complete and good
  answer.

The distinction is register, not content. The same sentence is help when it is
offered and damage when it is asserted.

## Why this is stricter than it sounds

This setting has forty years of tabletop material, three editions of rules, a
tie-in anime, and an enormous volume of confident community misinformation. A
plausible-sounding fact about Night City has a very high chance of being *from
somewhere* - which means an assistant generating one has a high chance of
generating something that sounds cited and is not.

And the cost lands on the player: they build a character around it, and find out
later. A player who catches you inventing one fact stops trusting the ones you
got right, which is the real damage.

## Scope

Method plus a list of in-game locations. No patch dependency. A lifepath-expanding
mod adds sources of its own and changes what source 1 shows.
