# The only firm dates near the heist are April and May 2077, and everything else is arithmetic

Three anchors, and they cluster:

| when | what |
|---|---|
| **April 2077** | Dex hires V and Jackie |
| **1 May 2077** | Saburo Arasaka arrives in Night City aboard the Kujira |
| **shortly after** | the Konpeki Plaza heist |

The game is set in **2077**, in **Night City**, in the Free State of North
California - not "in California", and the difference is a whole political history
that players who care about dates tend to care about too.

## Why this article exists

Because those three are load-bearing in a way that is easy to miss. **Every other
date anybody quotes around the prologue is derived from them**, by counting
backwards through a timeskip whose length the game never states.

That includes the calendar date of the corpo firing. There is no in-game document
that dates it. The dates the wikis carry are arithmetic: take the April 2077
hire, subtract somebody's estimate of the timeskip, publish the result as a fact.
The estimate is the soft part, and it disappears the moment the number is written
down without it.

See [What the game does not settle](/canon/what-the-game-does-not-settle) for the
timeskip itself, and
[Trace the footnote before repeating the wiki](/canon/trace-the-footnote-before-repeating-the-wiki)
for the general rule.

## How to use these when helping with a character

They are useful for **arithmetic the player is doing anyway**: how old a
character was during the Unification War, how long ago a job was, whether two
events could have overlapped. Do that arithmetic *with* the player rather than
for them, and show the anchor you started from, because that is what lets them
check you.

They are not useful as a way to settle a question the game left open. If the
arithmetic bottoms out in "it depends how long the timeskip was", say so and
stop, rather than picking a number to finish the sum.

## What is thinner than it looks

The **Kujira** as the name of Saburo's transport is the kind of detail that comes
from a single line or a single readable, and is the first thing to re-check if
somebody disputes it. The **date** is the durable part of that row; the vessel
name is the decorative part.

## Scope

Read off in-game quest text and readables. No patch dependency. Phantom Liberty
adds content in the same period without moving these anchors.
