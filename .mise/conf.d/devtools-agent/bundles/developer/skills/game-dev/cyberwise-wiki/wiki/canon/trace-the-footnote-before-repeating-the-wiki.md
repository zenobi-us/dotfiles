# Trace the footnote before repeating the wiki

**A wiki renders a documented fact and a fan's subtraction in the same font.**
That is the whole problem. "The heist takes place in 2077" and "the corpo firing
took place in 2076" sit in the same infobox, in the same weight, with the same
air of settledness - and one of them is read off the screen while the other is an
arithmetic result somebody published.

The defence is one click, and it is nearly always available: **follow the
citation.**

## The worked example

The first timeskip is universally given as "six months." Follow the footnote on
the wiki's own timeline entry and it resolves to a developer saying:

> "We did not specify the exact timeline - the first timeskip is a few months but
> less than a year."

The citation does not support the number. It **contradicts** it - it is a studio
declining to fix the value. And the "2076" attached to the pre-timeskip period is
that same developer's off-hand guess in the same sentence, promoted by repetition
into a date.

Nothing about the wiki page looked wrong. The number was plausible, consistent
with the other numbers on the page, and cited. It took opening the citation.

Full detail: [What the game does not settle](/canon/what-the-game-does-not-settle).

## The three categories, and the words for each

When a claim is about to be stated as canon, put it in one of three buckets and
use the matching language:

| bucket | what it means | how to say it |
|---|---|---|
| **documented** | on screen, in a shard, in quest text, spoken | "the game shows X" |
| **derived** | arithmetic or inference from documented anchors | "derived from X and Y - not documented" |
| **authorial** | a developer statement outside the game | "a developer said X; the game does not" |

The middle row is the one that gets lost, because a derivation produces a clean
number that looks exactly like a measurement. It is the same failure as
[a capacity read from the wrong API](/process/a-capacity-read-from-the-wrong-api):
the wrong answer arrives in the right units, of the right magnitude, and does not
announce itself.

**Do not silently collapse "derived" into "documented" to make an answer tidier.**
The person asking usually wanted the confidence level more than the number.

## Why this matters more here than for a mod question

A mod question has a filesystem behind it. When the docs and the disk disagree,
you open the file and the disagreement ends.

A canon question frequently has nothing behind it at all - the answer may not
exist. So there is no artefact to fall back on, and the only protection against
inventing one is being honest about which bucket the claim is in. **The absence
of an answer is itself the answer**, and it is a useful one to give a player,
because an open question is where their character goes.

## Practical checks

- **Open the citation before repeating the number.** Not the article - the
  citation.
- **Ask what an in-game document would have to look like** for the claim to be
  documented. A date implies a dated readable. If you cannot picture the artefact,
  it probably does not exist.
- **Watch for numbers that are differences.** Any claim of the form "X years
  before Y" is derived by construction.
- **Say "I am not certain" out loud when you are not.** It costs nothing in an
  interview and it is the only thing that keeps the facts you did get right worth
  anything.

## Scope

Method, not game data. No patch dependency. The worked example is one traced
footnote; the pattern was not surveyed across the wiki as a whole.
