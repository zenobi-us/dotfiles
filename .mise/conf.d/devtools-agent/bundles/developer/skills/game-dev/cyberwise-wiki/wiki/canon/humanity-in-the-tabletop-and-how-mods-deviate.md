# Humanity in the tabletop is permanent debt, not a meter that refills

The question "is this humanity mod lore-accurate?" cannot be answered without the
tabletop rules it is being measured against, and those rules are specific enough
that most people arguing about it are arguing from the video game instead.

## What Cyberpunk 2020 actually does

- **Humanity = Empathy x 10.** It is not an independent resource; it is a derived
  expression of one of the character's own statistics.
- **Every piece of cyberware carries a Humanity cost, paid at install.** Not
  accrued over time, not a risk roll - a price, charged once, at the ripperdoc.
- **Empathy falls as Humanity falls.** Because of the x10 relationship, losing
  Humanity degrades the stat itself, which degrades every social interaction the
  character has. The mechanical and the characterisational move together.
- **At zero, the character becomes an NPC.** A cyberpsycho, taken out of the
  player's hands. This is the origin of the specialist police unit that hunts
  them - the unit exists in the fiction because the rules produce those people.
- **The loss is permanent debt.** The only refund is therapy: expensive, slow, and
  something a character has to be able to afford in both money and downtime.

That last point carries the setting's actual argument, and it is the one that gets
lost:

> **Cyberpsychosis is partly a poverty disease.** The chrome is what the job
> demands; the therapy that offsets it is what only some people can buy. Two
> characters with identical implants end up in different places based on income.

And the framing the setting's creator has insisted on: **it is dissociative** - a
progressive detachment from other people as the capacity for empathy erodes - not
"metal makes you crazy." A mod, or a reading, that treats implants as a madness
meter has the mechanism wrong even when it has the numbers right.

## The yardstick this gives you

A humanity or cyberpsychosis mod for 2077 usually models the stat as a **renewable
meter**: it depletes with use or stress and refills with rest, drugs, sleep or
time.

That is a deliberate deviation, and it is worth naming precisely rather than
calling it inaccurate:

| tabletop | renewable-meter mod |
|---|---|
| erosion | fatigue |
| paid at install, permanently | spent by activity, recovered |
| refunded only by expensive therapy | refunded by rest or consumables |
| endpoint is losing the character | endpoint is a temporary state |

**Closer to fatigue than to erosion.** Neither is wrong as a design - a meter that
never comes back is punishing across a hundred-hour game, and the deviation is
usually made on purpose - but a player asking "is this lore-accurate" deserves the
comparison rather than a verdict.

Note also that 2077's own [Cyberware Capacity is the vestige of this
system](/gameplay/cyberware-capacity-is-the-humanity-stat): the internal stat names
are still `Humanity*`, and what they now measure is a budget with no psychological
dimension at all. The video game had already made the deviation before any mod did.

## Scope

Tabletop rules for Cyberpunk 2020 plus the creator's public framing of what
cyberpsychosis is. This is **setting material, not read off the 2077 screen** - it
is here as the yardstick for judging a mod's claim, not as a statement about what
2077 establishes. Edition differences exist; 2020 is what is described above, and
later editions rework it.
