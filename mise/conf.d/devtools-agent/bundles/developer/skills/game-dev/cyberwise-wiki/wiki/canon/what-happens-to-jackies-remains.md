# What you do with Jackie's body gates a later sidejob

After the heist, V decides what happens to Jackie's remains. The choice looks like
flavour and is not: it decides whether a later sidejob - the ofrenda held by his
family - happens at all.

| what V does with the body | the ofrenda sidejob |
|---|---|
| **send it to his family** | unlocked |
| **leave it / send it nowhere** | unlocked |
| **send it to the ripperdoc** | **not** unlocked |

The third row is the one nobody expects, and it is the finding.

## Why the ripperdoc route was believed to work

Both the assistant answering and the player asking initially believed that sending
the body to the ripperdoc also led to the ofrenda, and the belief is a reasonable
one. The ripperdoc is Jackie's oldest friend and effectively family; handing the
body to him reads as the most respectful of the three options, and in a game that
generally rewards loyalty it is the choice a player expects to be rewarded.

**The game's own text settled it**, and what it says is darker than the assumption:
the ripperdoc does not keep the body. It is later revealed that **the corporation
took the body from him** - so the option that felt like giving Jackie to the people
who loved him ends with Arasaka holding his remains, and there is no funeral to
attend.

That reframing is the reason the fact is worth recording rather than just the
unlock table. The choice is not "family versus friend"; it is "family versus a
friend who cannot protect it."

corrective - what the user saw in game outranks what you remember - was not
available. When both parties share an assumption, the only thing that breaks the tie
is the artefact, and in a canon question the artefact is a line of text somebody has
to actually go and read.

## What this constrains in a character document

A backstory or a played-through account that has V attending the ofrenda has
implicitly made the body choice, and it was not the ripperdoc. If a player's
narrative wants both - the body given to the ripperdoc *and* the ofrenda - that is
a deliberate departure, which is fine as long as they chose it. See
[Jackie dies before Johnny exists](/canon/jackie-dies-before-johnny-exists) for the
ordering constraints around the same stretch of story.

## Scope

Read off the game's own quest text and the sidejob's unlock behaviour. Single
determination, but of a documented, on-screen fact. Story-altering and quest-restoring
mods change unlock conditions; ask before assuming a player's account is wrong.
