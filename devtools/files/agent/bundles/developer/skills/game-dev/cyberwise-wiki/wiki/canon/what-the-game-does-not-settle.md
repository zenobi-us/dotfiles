# What the game does not settle, and what gets stated as canon anyway

**A confidently stated non-fact is worse than an admitted gap**, because the gap
is where the player's character goes. Four of these come up constantly, and none
of them has an in-game source.

For each one below: what people say, and what it actually rests on.

---

## 1. The length of the first timeskip

**What gets said:** "six months."

**What the game says:** nothing. There is no title card, no number, no dated
document on either side of it. The montage of V and Jackie working together runs
and the story resumes.

"Six months" is **wiki gloss**. Follow the wiki's own footnote on the timeline
and it lands on a developer statement:

> "We did not specify the exact timeline - the first timeskip is a few months but
> less than a year."[^dev-timeskip]

Two things about that quote are worth reading twice. It is a developer saying the
studio **deliberately declined to fix it** - so it is not that the answer is
undocumented, it is that there is no answer. And even the "2076" that gets
attached to the pre-timeskip period is that same developer's off-hand guess in
the same breath, not a date from the game.

**So the honest answer is a range with a stated source:** a few months, under a
year, per the developers, and the player picks. Which is a better answer for
somebody writing a character than "six months" ever was, because it hands them
the choice.

[^dev-timeskip]: Developer statement, quoted as the footnote behind the community
wiki's own timeline entry. Reproduced here because the footnote is the whole
point: the wiki's confident date resolves to a developer saying there is no date.

## 2. The calendar date of the corpo firing

**What gets said:** a specific month and year.

**What it rests on:** arithmetic. Take the April 2077 hire
([the dates the game puts on the board](/canon/the-dates-the-game-puts-on-the-board)),
subtract somebody's estimate of the timeskip above, publish the difference.

There is **no in-game document that dates the firing.** The uncertainty in item 1
propagates straight into this number, and then vanishes, because a subtraction
produces a clean-looking date with no error bar attached to it.

## 3. How corpo V and Jackie became friends

**What gets said:** an origin story. Often a good one.

**What the game says:** that they are already close when the prologue opens. That
is the entire content.

This is genuinely unwritten space rather than sparse documentation - there is no
shard, no line and no scene about it, and the game clearly means the player to
have it. It is arguably the richest unclaimed ground a corpo V has.

**It is also where the assistant working from these sessions failed.** It
invented a ten-year friendship with a bar-fight origin, offered it as background
rather than as a suggestion, and had to retract it. The failure was not the idea -
it was the register. An idea offered as an idea is help; the same sentence stated
as background overwrites the player's character with your own.

---

## 4. V's age

**What gets said:** a number, usually mid-to-late twenties.

**What the game says:** nothing. Age is not set at character creation, not stated
in dialogue, and not derivable from any document. It belongs to the player along
with name, gender and look.

Ask, do not supply. If a player wants V to have been an adult during the
Unification War, do the arithmetic with them from
[the anchors](/canon/the-dates-the-game-puts-on-the-board) rather than telling
them it is impossible.

---

## The one exception in the other direction: the first names

**V's default first names are fixed: Vincent and Valerie.** This is the game's one
uncustomisable naming constraint, and CDPR locked it so the recorded voice lines
work - characters say the name out loud. The surname is the player's; the default
given name is not.

It is worth knowing precisely because it inverts the pattern of this article:
almost everything about V is the player's, and this narrow thing is not.

## The rule this all points at

When a date or a number is about to be stated as canon, **find what the claim
rests on first**. Say "derived, not documented" when that is the answer. The
method is one article over:
[Trace the footnote before repeating the wiki](/canon/trace-the-footnote-before-repeating-the-wiki).

## Scope

The four negatives are claims about the absence of a source, and a negative is
only as wide as the search behind it - these were checked against the intro
sequences, quest text, shards and the wiki's own citations. If somebody produces
an in-game readable that dates the firing, this article is what is wrong.
