# Violence against civilians is economically inert

In the vanilla game, killing a civilian yields:

- **no loot** - bystander NPCs carry no lootable inventory
- **no money** - no eddies drop
- **police attention**, which costs time and ammunition

That is the entire ledger. The sandbox **permits** the act and takes deliberate
care that it never becomes a **strategy**. It is not blocked, not scripted against,
not punished narratively - it is simply made worthless, which is a quieter and more
robust piece of design than a prohibition would be.

## Why this is the useful framing

"Can I do it" and "is it worth doing" are different questions, and only the second
one has a mechanical answer here. A player asking the first will be told yes by
anyone who has played five minutes. The answer that actually changes their
behaviour is: *yes, and the game will pay you nothing.*

It also settles a class of question that comes up when someone is planning a
character with an ugly streak. The game does not stop them, does not score them,
and does not remember. Whatever weight the act carries is the player's to supply -
which is the same shape as most of [what the game does not settle](/canon/what-the-game-does-not-settle).

## The diagnostic value

This is the reason the fact is worth writing down at all.

> **If a player reports that harming civilians costs them something - eddies,
> reputation, a stat, a humanity meter, a vendor refusing to trade - that effect is
> in the mod layer. Vanilla has nothing to produce it.**

So the search starts at the mods that hook the crime or reputation systems, not at
the game's own records. It is a clean instance of the general rule that
[a negative is only as wide as the layer you searched](/process/an-empty-result-is-not-a-finding):
"the game does nothing here" is a strong claim, and it is defensible in this one
case precisely because the vanilla behaviour has been checked.

Pair it with
[a yellow-marked hostile is never a civilian](/gameplay/a-marked-hostile-is-never-a-civilian):
together they say what such a mod actually taxes, which is crossfire rather than
choice of target.

## Scope

Read off the vanilla sandbox. No loot table was traced to confirm the absence at
the record level - the observation is that nothing drops and nothing is lootable,
which is the level the question is asked at. Police response behaviour has been
reworked between patches; the loot outcome has not changed.
