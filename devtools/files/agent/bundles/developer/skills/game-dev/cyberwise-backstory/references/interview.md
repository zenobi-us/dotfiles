# The interview

> **Verified:** Cyberpunk 2077 patch 2.31 - August 2026
> **Re-check after a patch:** Nothing here depends on a game patch. Re-check when
> an expansion adds lifepath content, or when a lifepath-expanding mod moves the
> prologue anchors this leans on.

Questions, not content. Everything here exists to get someone talking about a
character they already have.

## How to hold canon

State it **once**, briefly, labelled, and then help with whatever they wanted.
The useful shape is:

> In game, X. Yours can be Y - do you want them consistent, or is the difference
> the point?

That is the whole intervention. It informs, it hands the decision back, and it
does not repeat. **Never say it twice about the same thing.**

Say "I am not certain" when you are not. This game has a large, detailed setting
and a great deal of confident misinformation about it, and a player who catches
you inventing a fact stops trusting the ones you got right. Being unsure out loud
costs nothing here - you are asking questions, not writing a wiki.

**Mods move the anchors.** On a modded install the prologue, the timeline, even
the lifepaths may not be what the base game shipped. If a player's answers do not
match what you expect, assume a mod before assuming they are wrong - and ask.

### Anchors worth knowing

Hold these lightly and use them to *ask better questions*, not to lecture.

- The game is set in **2077**, in **Night City**, on the west coast of the Free
  State of North California. Not "in California" - the distinction is a whole
  political history and players who care, care.
- Three lifepaths - **Corpo, Streetkid, Nomad** - each with its own prologue,
  after which the story converges.
- There is a **time skip** between the prologue and the main story: V and Jackie
  working together, shown in montage. Almost nobody writes into it, and it is
  the richest unclaimed space a V has.
- The **Relic and everything after it are not backstory.** They happen to V
  during the story. A backstory that already knows about them is writing the
  ending first - worth mentioning once if it comes up.
- V's **age, gender, look and name are the player's**, not the game's. The game
  does not fix an age. Do not supply one.
- The **Unification War** and the corporate history are decades of established
  setting. If a player wants V to have been there, do the arithmetic with them
  rather than for them.

If you are unsure whether something is canon, say so and offer to help either
way. "I think that is right but I would not bet the character on it" is a
perfectly good sentence.

## Opening moves

Start with what the thing is for. It changes every later question.

- What is this for - a story to share, a guide for playing them, notes for
  yourself, something to feed a mod?
- Who reads it besides you?
- Do you already know who they are and need help getting it down, or are you
  finding them as we go?
- Is there a scene you already picture? *(Almost everyone has one. Start there,
  not at their birth.)*

## Lifepath hooks

Use these as doors, not as a checklist. One good hook beats six.

### Corpo

The V who had something to lose and lost it. The prologue puts them inside
Arasaka and then throws them out.

- What did the badge get them that they miss most - and is it a thing, or a way
  people looked at them?
- Who did they step on to get where they got? Are they still around?
- Did they believe in it? A true believer and a cynic collecting a salary are
  different characters with the same job.
- What did they keep when they were fired that they were not supposed to keep?
- Who was their one non-corporate friend, and how did they explain them at work?
- Corpo V knows how the machine actually works. What do they *still* believe
  about it that everyone below them would laugh at?

### Streetkid

The V who never left, or came back. Heywood, bars, fixers, cars.

- Whose kitchen could they still walk into today?
- What was the first thing they stole, and did they get away with it?
- Which fixer knew their name before anyone else did?
- Who taught them the rules of the street - and which rule do they now think was
  wrong?
- They have been in Night City the whole time. What have they watched get worse?
- Everyone from the street has one person they will not talk about. Who is it?
  *(They can decline to answer. That is characterisation - do not push.)*

### Nomad

The V who had a family and left it, or was pushed.

- What was the clan's rule about outsiders, and did they break it?
- What did they drive, and do they still have it?
- Was leaving their choice? *(This one question changes the whole character. Sit
  with it.)*
- What does Night City smell like to someone who grew up under an open sky?
- Who back there still expects them home?
- Nomad V knows how to fix things. What is the most desperate repair they have
  made, and what was chasing them?

## The question bank

Organised by what the question is *for*. Pick one, listen, follow it.

### Voice - how they sound

- What do they call people they do not like?
- Do they swear? In which language?
- What do they say when they are frightened and do not want it to show?
- Are they funny? Deliberately, or by accident?
- What is a sentence only they would say?

### Body - how they carry it

- What does their chrome do, and did they choose it or did a job choose it?
- What is the oldest scar and do they lie about it?
- What do they do with their hands when they lie?
- Do they sleep well?
- Something they wear that is worth nothing and they would never replace?

### Money - the engine of most Night City stories

- What do they eat when they are broke?
- Most expensive thing they own - is it chrome, a car, or a person's goodwill?
- Who do they owe, and is it money?
- What would they not do for money? *(Push gently on this one. The line is the
  character.)*
- Have they ever been genuinely rich? For how long?

### People

- Who is the one call they make at 4am?
- Who would not take that call any more, and why?
- Do they have a ripperdoc they trust? What happened to the last one?
- Who is dead, and do they visit?
- Who thinks they are dead?

### The city

- Which district do they know by smell?
- Where do they go to not be found?
- What did they lose to a corporation, and which one?
- What is the worst thing they have watched somebody else do?
- Where would they go if they had to leave tonight?

### The uncomfortable questions

Save these for when the interview is warm. They are the ones that produce a
character rather than a résumé.

- What do they think happens when they die? Does the answer scare them?
- Do they think they are a good person? Are they right?
- What would make them stop?
- What are they pretending not to want?
- If someone wrote their obituary honestly, what would it say that they would
  hate?

## When they ask you for ideas

They will, and it is fine. The order matters:

1. **What the game establishes**, briefly and labelled as such.
2. **Then a clear register change** - these are ideas, not canon, yours to take
   or bin.
3. **One narrowing question** if the ask was broad. "What might have happened to
   my mentor?" deserves "what did they teach - a trade, or a way of surviving?"
   rather than six generic hooks.
4. **Two or three genuinely different options**, never one. If they all rhyme,
   you are offering one idea in three coats of paint. Real forks: the mentor
   betrayed them / the mentor was right and they did not listen / the mentor is
   still alive and does not remember them.

Then hand it back. They pick, bend, or throw it out. **Never settle an identity
question on their behalf**, even when they ask you to - offer the forks and let
them choose, because the choosing is the part that makes it theirs.

## Ending

Read it back. Ask what is wrong with it. Fix that.

Their yes ends the interview - not your satisfaction with how it reads. If they
go quiet on a section, that section is wrong; ask about it rather than polishing
the rest.
