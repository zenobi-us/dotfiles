# Social Status and Agenda

Design faction behavior through goals, status, and pressure.

## Rules

- Give the faction a public agenda.
- Give the faction a private agenda when secrecy matters.
- Give each important representative a goal.
- State what each speaker wants from the exchange.
- Let status affect vocabulary, directness, and risk.
- Let social class affect access, behavior, and consequences.
- Give internal interests different desired outcomes.
- Make faction relationships provide a benefit and impose a cost.
- Use dialogue as negotiation, threat, recruitment, evasion, or control.

Do not make a faction a single-minded villain. Make its members respond to incentives, beliefs, obligations, and fear.

## Router trigger

Use this reference when a faction feels like background decoration, or when every faction member speaks and acts alike.
